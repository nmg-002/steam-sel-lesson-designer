# The Living Market: Supply and Demand × Drama × SEL
Subject: Introductory Economics | Concept: Supply, demand and equilibrium | First-year university | 60 min | Groups of 6–8 | EMC²: Empathy · Critical Inquiry · Compassion

**1. Objectives and success criteria**
Students will be able to explain how markets reach equilibrium by role-playing buyers and sellers, then mapping the experience onto supply and demand curves.
- I can predict how a change in demand or supply shifts equilibrium price and quantity. *(economics)*
- I can act out a buyer's or seller's decision and explain the reasoning behind it. *(drama)*
- I can describe how the same price change affects different people differently. *(SEL)*

**Materials:** buyer and seller role cards (budget or minimum price), paper, pens, projector. **No-tech:** draw the curves on the board.

**2. Introduction (5 + 10 min)**
- *Hook (5):* "Why does bottled water cost more at a crowded festival?" Take three answers.
- *Concept (10):* demand, supply and equilibrium using the market for cups of chai on campus. Distinguish a movement along a curve from a shift of the curve.

**3. Activity: The Living Market (20 min)**
- *Round 1, a normal day:* buyers and sellers negotiate one cup of chai each; observers record the agreed prices.
- *Round 2, demand rises:* "A festival brings crowds to campus." Trade again.
- *Round 3, supply falls:* "A milk shortage cuts the chai stalls' output." Trade again.
- Roles: buyer, seller, observer or note-taker. Nobody has to perform, and nobody needs acting skill.
- **Tiers:** *Support:* label a prepared graph. *Core:* draw the shift for each round. *Stretch:* analyse a round where demand and supply change at the same time, and say what can't be determined.
- **Quick check:** "Which curve shifted in Round 3, and which way?"

**4. Graphing and SEL debrief (10 + 10 min)**
- Students turn the three rounds into supply and demand diagrams, then compare them with the observers' price records.

| Pillar | How | Debrief question |
|---|---|---|
| Empathy | Stepping into buyer and seller roles | How did your role's budget or costs shape your choices? |
| Critical Inquiry | Comparing the role-play with the model | Where did real behaviour differ from the curves, and why? |
| Compassion | Considering buyers who were priced out | Who missed out when prices rose, and what could a fair campus do? |

**5. Close (5 min)**
- Exit ticket: "Production costs rise. What happens to equilibrium price and quantity?" *(Answer: supply shifts left; price rises, quantity falls.)*
- Peer check against the "I can" statements.
- **Transfer:** notice one price change this week and sketch which curve moved.

**6. Share:** post the class's best diagrams on the course forum.
