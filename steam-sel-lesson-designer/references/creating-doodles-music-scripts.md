# Creating doodles, music, soundscapes and scripts

Read this when the educator wants a finished creative resource rather than a plan: a doodle to show the class, a song or chant, a soundscape, or a script. Everything here follows the handbook: simple, low-cost, no artistic skill needed, accurate content, and room for learners to make their own versions.

**Contents:** 1. Doodles · 2. Songs, chants and rhythms · 3. Soundscape scripts · 4. Drama scripts · 5. Using other tools and existing resources · 6. Credits for created resources

## 1. Doodles

**Always give a drawing guide first**, so any educator can draw it on a board: basic shapes → elements → combine → labels and speech bubbles → a dash of colour (see Output B in `output-templates.md`).

**If your platform can create images**, offer to draw the doodle as well. Keep to the handbook's doodle style:
- simple black lines on a white background, a little colour for emphasis, no photorealism;
- personified elements with clear faces and feelings, short speech or thought bubbles, arrows and labels (for example the leaf as a chef, the "gas gang" in the Greenhouse Party, Auntie Mangrove);
- labels spelled correctly in the chosen output language, and scientifically accurate details (check proportions, order of stages, chemical formulas);
- age-appropriate, with diverse, non-stereotyped characters.

**Directly under every image, always add this "image card"**, in the same answer, without waiting to be asked. Testing showed these three lines are easy to forget, and they matter for accessibility, accuracy and honesty:
```
🖼️ Image card
Description: [2–4 sentences describing the doodle for learners who can't see it, as the handbook describes its own doodles]
Fact check: [confirm the labels and science are correct, or list anything to fix; AI images often misspell labels or get details wrong]
AI-generated doodle, created with [tool name, if known]. Shared under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 where the tool's terms allow.
```
Ask the image tool for the handbook's simple doodle style (black lines, a little colour, white background), not a polished illustration.

**If your platform can't create images**, give the drawing guide plus a short, ready-to-paste **image prompt** the educator can use in an image tool, for example: *"Simple hand-drawn doodle, black ink with soft green and yellow, white background: a smiling leaf wearing a chef's hat stirring a pot labelled CO₂ + H₂O, a cheerful sun saying 'Here's some energy!', a sugar cube character labelled glucose, and O₂ bubbles floating up. Clear printed labels, no other text."* Remind them to check the tool's licence terms (section 5).

**Learners first.** A teacher-made doodle is a model or a hook. The handbook's goal is learners making their own, so always pair it with learner doodle prompts.

## 2. Songs, chants and rhythms

You may write **original** songs, chants, rhymes, raps or verses about the concept. Never reproduce or adapt copyrighted lyrics; you may only *suggest* a copyrighted song, labelled as such (see `research-and-licensing.md`).

For each song, include **all** of the following in your **first answer**, not as later options. Testing showed that without this, the melody and rhythm get left out:
- **Lyrics:** 2–4 short verses and a chorus, accurate content, age-appropriate vocabulary, in the output language chosen in question 13c. Mark key subject terms in bold.
- **A simple melody** that teachers can sing without reading staff notation. Use **sargam** (Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni Sa) where it fits the context, or doh-re-mi, one syllable per note, with dashes for held notes, for example `Sa Re Ga – | Ga Re Sa – |`. Keep to five or six notes and a small range so everyone can sing it.
- **Or a familiar open tune:** you may suggest singing the words to a **traditional or public-domain** tune. Ask the educator to verify that the tune is genuinely traditional or public domain in their country, and never use a copyrighted pop or film melody.
- **Body percussion:** a 4-beat rhythm pattern for learners without instruments, for example `Clap · Clap · Snap · Stamp`, and a way to layer it (one group claps, one group sings).
- **Everyday-object instruments:** suggest a water xylophone, bottle shakers, desk drumming or seed rattles, as in the handbook.
- **SEL link:** one EMC² question about the song (for example "How did singing it together feel?").
- **Learner version:** invite learners to write their own verse, as in the handbook's "rewrite a verse" activity.
- **Science check of the lyrics:** song imagery must not teach a misconception. For example, water vapour is invisible, so don't describe rising vapour as "little clouds"; clouds form only when vapour condenses into droplets. Fix any line that bends the facts, or add a one-line note for the teacher.

For **data sonification**, give the data table, the note each value maps to (higher value = higher note), and how to play it on a water xylophone, sargam or a free online keyboard. Use real data only from a source you opened, or mark it "(please verify)".

## 3. Soundscape scripts

A soundscape script is a timed "sound score" that learners perform live with voices, bodies and objects, like the handbook's thunder-and-footsteps storytelling track or the Monsoon Then and Now soundscape.

Write it as a table:

| Time | Sound | How to make it | Who | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Light rain | Fingertips tapping desks, slowly building | Group A | Soft, growing |
| 0:20–0:30 | Thunder | Shaking a sheet of chart paper | One volunteer | Loud |
| … | … | … | … | … |

Then add:
- the **mood arc** (for example calm → tension → calm) and what it shows about the concept;
- a **narration line** or two to read over it, if useful;
- a **mindfulness moment:** eyes closed, listen again, "What do you notice now?";
- options for learners with hearing impairments (visual cues, vibration, a conductor's gestures) and for learners who prefer not to perform (conductor, timekeeper, recorder).

If the educator wants recorded sounds, propose **openly licensed sound libraries first** (for example Wikimedia Commons, Internet Archive, Freesound, Free Music Archive), with the licence of each sound.

## 4. Drama scripts

Write complete, original scripts as in Output B (`output-templates.md`): monologue, dialogue, narration or a tableau sequence, with stage directions in *(italics)*, characters whose traits match **accurate** properties of the concept, a fact-check box, staging tips for an ordinary classroom, and reflection-journal prompts. Also:
- give an **approximate performance time**;
- offer **roles for learners who don't want to act** (narrator, sound effects, director, prop maker);
- keep sensitive topics fictional or third-person, with opt-outs (see the guardrails in `SKILL.md`);
- for historical or real people, keep facts accurate and mark invented dialogue as imagined.

## 5. Using other tools and existing resources

- **Open resources first:** when suggesting existing images, music, sounds or videos, follow the OER-first order in `research-and-licensing.md`: openly licensed (Creative Commons) resources first, public domain next with a request to verify its status and authenticity, and copyrighted material only as a clearly labelled suggestion that the educator must check for access and accessibility.
- **AI image or music tools:** if the educator wants an AI tool to make an image or a recording, give them a clear prompt and remind them to **read the tool's licence and terms first**. Some tools restrict how their output can be shared or used, and that matters because materials shared from this skill should carry CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Suggest checking that the output doesn't imitate a recognisable artist, song or copyrighted character.
- **Never** claim a resource is free, open or public domain unless you saw its licence.

## 6. Credits for created resources

Add to the end of every created doodle, song, soundscape or script:
- **What it is and who made it:** for example *"Original song created with AI assistance using the STEAM-SEL Lesson Designer skill"* or *"AI-generated doodle (image created with [tool name, if known])"*.
- **Licence:** *"Shared under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, where the AI tool's terms allow."*
- **TASL credits** for any open or public-domain resources reused, and *"© Copyrighted, suggestion only"* labels for any copyrighted resources mentioned.
- The usual handbook credit and AI disclaimer (see `SKILL.md`, section 7).
