# Output templates

Read the template for the requested output before writing it. Use Markdown headings and tables, and offer to reformat for printing (no tables) or slides.

**Contents:** A Lesson plan · B Educator-created resource · C Learner worksheet · D Multi-session unit · E Quick starter · F Feedback on a plan · G SDG Lens plan · H Export formats · I Quiz · J Levelled text · K Sharing pack · L Learner rubric · Worked example

## Output A: Lesson Plan (Mode 2: learners create; mirrors the handbook template)
Write it in the language(s) chosen in question 13c (for a bilingual plan, put the two languages side by side or one under the other, with a glossary of key terms), and at the length the educator chose in question 13a:
- **Compact outline:** about 600 words (roughly one printed page). Keep every step below, but in short lines: one line per tier, 3–4 prompts, one debrief question per EMC² pillar used, and the 4-line Quality Check.
- **Detailed version:** about two printed pages, adding teacher notes, sample scripts or examples, fuller differentiation and needs-specific adjustments. The Quality Check stays at four lines.

## Output B: Educator-Created Resource (Mode 1)
For finished doodles (including images, where the platform can make them), original songs and chants, soundscape scripts and drama scripts, also follow `creating-doodles-music-scripts.md`.
- **Doodle:** Subject, concept and grade; 3–5 key elements; relatability device; a **step-by-step drawing guide** (basic shapes, then elements, then combine, then labels and bubbles, then a dash of colour), written so a non-artist can draw it on a board; the speech-bubble text; evaluation; 5–9 discussion questions spread across EMC²; ways learners can extend the doodle.
- **Soundtrack / soundscape cue sheet:** a table with columns `Time | Lesson moment | Sound/music type to source (or make) | Intended mood | Volume | Teacher action`; search keywords for open repositories; a DIY-sound alternative; an evaluation (ambience right? invites reflection?).
- **Script:** title; characters (with personality traits tied to **accurate** concept properties); setting; a monologue, dialogue, narration or tableau sequence, with stage directions in *(italics)*; a fact-check box mapping each line to the concept; reflection-journal prompts; staging tips for a normal classroom.

## Output C: Learner Worksheet
Learner-friendly title; a short "What you need"; a quick model or example; prompt boxes with space; a feelings or emotion word bank (age-appropriate); a self-reflection section ("What did you feel? What new idea came to mind?"); a 2-item self-check. Note in brackets where blank drawing or writing space should go.

## Output D: Multi-session Unit
A table: `Session | Focus concept | Art form | EMC² pillar | Activity | Output | Minutes`. Build up from doodle (understand), to music (feel and context), to drama (perspective and action), and finish with a learner-led showcase. Then a full Output A for session 1, and the others on request.

## Output G: SDG Lens Plan (Mode 4; mirrors the Chapter 5 templates)
**G1, educator applies the SDG Lens** (a resource the educator creates):
```
# [Issue] through [Art form(s)]: SDG Lens
Step 1 · Spot the issue: Subject … | Concept … | Grade/Age … | Real-world issue …
Step 2 · Local story: whose life it touches, close to home …
Step 3 · Map the SDGs: Main SDG … | Connected SDGs …
Step 4 · Art form(s) and creation: ☐ Doodle ☐ Music/soundscape ☐ Drama
         (describe the creation: doodle guide / cue sheet / script)
Step 5 · Build EMC²
  Check: accurate? ☐ | connects to learners' lives? ☐ | reflective without undue distress? ☐ | points to hope or action? ☐
  | Empathy | Mindfulness | Critical Inquiry | Compassion |  (one question each)
Step 6 · Act and share: use as ☐ intro ☐ review ☐ discussion prompt ☐ call to action
  One action learners will take: … | Who they will share it with: …
```
**G2, learners take creative action** (lesson plan): the Output A structure with these changes:
- Header adds **SDG(s)**.
- Step 2 introduction **starts from learners' own lives** (a local change, a news story, a memory, a person they know).
- Step 3 lets learners **choose** an art form (some prefer to doodle rather than perform).
- Step 4 names the SEL integration **and one concrete action**.
- Step 5 adds "Does it balance honesty about the issue with hope and agency?" and monitors "engaged, or anxious and withdrawn? Did it end on a hopeful note?"
- Step 6 suggests sharing beyond the classroom (assembly, parents, panchayat or municipal council).
- Include an **age-adaptation** box (5–8 / 8–11 / 14–18) and a **"Swap the issue"** line showing how the same plan works for another SDG.

## Output H: Export formats
On request, re-present any output as:
- **Printable:** no tables wider than the page, clear headings, blank space marked for drawing or writing, black-and-white friendly.
- **Slide outline:** one slide per lesson phase, with a title, 3–5 bullet points, a speaker note and a suggestion for an image or doodle (with a licence note if it's not the educator's own).
- **Word / Google Docs:** clean headings and tables that paste well; suggest "File › Download" to save as .docx or PDF.
- **WhatsApp / SMS (low bandwidth):** plain text only, no tables, short lines, emojis as section markers, under about 1,500 characters per message, split into numbered messages if needed. Keep the credit and AI disclaimer in the last message.
You can't create files yourself unless your platform allows it; if not, give the formatted text for the educator to copy.

## Output I: Starter or exit quiz
3–6 questions matched to the success criteria and learner stage: a mix of multiple-choice, true/false-with-reason and one open question linked to the art activity ("Point to / describe the part of your doodle that shows…"). Give an **answer key** and, for each wrong option, the **likely misconception** and a one-line fix. For early years, use pictures, gestures or oral questions instead.

## Output J: Level a text
Take a passage (the educator's own, or one you write) and rewrite it at **2–3 reading levels** (e.g. early reader, core, advanced). Keep the same facts and key vocabulary in every version, with a short glossary for the simplest level. Only level text the educator owns or that is openly licensed for adaptation (see `research-and-licensing.md`); never level a text marked ND or all rights reserved.

## Output K: Prepare for sharing as an open resource
The handbook invites educators to share their creations with the community. Produce a sharing pack:
- **Title** and a **2–3 sentence description** (topic, learner stage, art form, SEL focus, SDG if any).
- **Licence label:** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, with the link, because it adapts the handbook.
- **Credits:** the educator as author; the handbook; this skill, using the line *"Created with the STEAM-SEL Lesson Designer skill by NMG (2026), created under the mentorship of EO as part of the OE4BW 2026 programme, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Source: https://github.com/nmg-002/steam-sel-lesson-designer"*; TASL credits for every third-party resource; an AI-assistance note.
- **Keywords** (subject, stage, art form, EMC², SDG) and **what's included** (plan, worksheet, script, cue sheet).
- **A check before sharing:** no learner names, faces or personal data; all third-party resources licensed for sharing; facts verified.
Remind the educator they can share it with the handbook's community repository, if one is available to them.

## Output L: Learner rubric
A 3-level table (*Getting there · Got it · Going further*) with one row each for: the subject concept; using the art form to show understanding (not artistic skill); SEL reflection; and, if relevant, working with others. Write each cell as a learner-friendly description. For ages 5–8, use faces or pictures and 2–3 rows only.

## Worked example (for calibration; adapt, don't copy)

**Brief:** Grade 5, 32 learners, rural school, Konkani/English, two learners with low vision, no internet, 40 min, Science: Germination (new concept), learners create, groups of 4.
**Recommendation:** Doodling. Germination is a staged process with interacting factors, and personifying the seed builds empathy and an understanding of perseverance. For the low-vision learners: thick black markers, large paper, tactile seed samples, and a "describer" role in their group.
**Excerpt of Step 3 prompts:**
1. Doodle water, sunlight and soil as characters talking to the seed. What does each one say?
2. Draw the seed's feelings at each stage: sleepy, determined, triumphant.
3. What qualities does the seed show? Is it a warrior breaking its shell, or a dancer reaching for the sun?
4. Show how sun, soil and water support the sapling. Add one helper (a pebble?) the seed asks for support.
**EMC²:** Empathy (the seed's struggle), Mindfulness (slow, close observation of the stages), Critical Inquiry (why do some seeds sprout faster?), Compassion (parallels with people facing challenges).
**Timing:** 5 hook (teacher doodle) + 8 concept + 5 modelling + 14 group doodling + 8 gallery walk and debrief = 40 ✔
