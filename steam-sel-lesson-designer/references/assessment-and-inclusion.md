# Assessment, differentiation and inclusion

Read this before writing a plan or resource.

**Contents:** 1. Assessing learning in the subject and the art form · 2. UDL 3.0 check · 3. Three-tier differentiation · 4. EMC² and CASEL crosswalk · 5. Diversity and inclusion checklist

## 1. Assessing learning in both the subject and the art form
In arts integration, learners build and show their understanding **through** the art form, so objectives and assessment cover **both** the subject concept and the art form (Kennedy Center definition of arts integration). The handbook's Single-Point Rubric (the Single-Point Rubric in `handbook-knowledge.md`) is for the **educator's** self-assessment. Learners need their own success criteria.

- **Success criteria:** turn each objective into 2–4 learner-friendly **"I can…"** statements, at least one for the subject, one for the art form, and one for SEL. Example (germination doodle): "I can show the stages of germination in order" · "I can use faces and speech bubbles to show how the seed feels" · "I can explain how sun, soil and water help the seed."
- **Assess understanding, never artistic talent.** A messy doodle that shows the concept correctly meets the criteria; a beautiful one that doesn't, doesn't. Say this to learners.
- **Checks during the lesson:** plan 1–2 quick checks (thumbs up/sideways/down, a one-word whip-round, "point to the part of your doodle that shows…", a freeze-frame that must show a key idea).
- **Learner rubric (Output L):** a simple 3-level table (*Getting there · Got it · Going further*) for the subject, the art form as a way of showing understanding, and SEL reflection. For early years, use pictures or faces instead of words.
- **Self- and peer assessment:** "Two stars and a wish" or Glows and Grows, using the success criteria.
- **Quizzes (Output I):** a starter quiz to check prior knowledge and an exit quiz to check learning, with answer keys and the likely misconceptions behind wrong answers.
- **Evidence:** suggest simple, low-cost ways to keep evidence (photos of doodles, a short audio clip of a soundscape, a script, a reflection journal), with learner consent and no faces or names if shared.

## 2. Universal Design for Learning (UDL Guidelines 3.0)
CAST's UDL Guidelines 3.0 (2024) aim to grow **learner agency** and address barriers rooted in bias and exclusion. Apply a quick UDL check to every plan:
- **Engagement:** does the plan build belonging, affirm learners' identities, offer real choice, and leave room for joy and empathy?
- **Representation:** is the concept presented in more than one way (visual, sound, movement, words), with diverse perspectives and ways of knowing, and with biased presentations questioned rather than repeated?
- **Action and expression:** can learners show their understanding in more than one way (doodle, sound, movement, speech, writing, a home language), including communication styles that have often been marginalised?
Arts integration already supports UDL. Say how in one line ("UDL check: …") in the Quality Check.

## 3. Differentiation in three tiers
For every main activity, offer three tiers, with the **same concept and success criteria** but different scaffolding:
- **Support:** a partly drawn doodle template, sentence starters, word bank with pictures, a given sound sequence, a script with gaps, a partner role.
- **Core:** the main prompts as planned.
- **Stretch:** add a second concept, critique the metaphor, compose an original verse, write from a second perspective, or link to data.
Let learners choose their tier where possible, and never label learners publicly. For any text learners must read, offer to **level it** (Output J): the same content at 2–3 reading levels, with key vocabulary kept.

## 4. EMC² and CASEL (optional crosswalk)
Many schools report SEL against CASEL's five competencies. Use EMC² as the design lens, and show this crosswalk **only if** the educator asks or mentions CASEL:

| EMC² | Closest CASEL competencies |
|---|---|
| Empathy | Social awareness · Relationship skills |
| Mindfulness | Self-awareness · Self-management |
| Critical Inquiry | Responsible decision-making |
| Compassion | Social awareness · Relationship skills · Responsible decision-making |

The match is approximate; the frameworks overlap rather than map one-to-one. Say so if you show it. For SEL, also plan a short **transfer** moment: "Where else could you use this skill this week?"

## 5. Diversity and inclusion checklist (apply to every output)
- **Representation:** across characters, examples, scientists, historical figures, names and stories, include a mix of genders, cultures, regions (Global South and North), languages, ages, abilities, and economic and family backgrounds. Include contributions often missing from textbooks (women, Indigenous and local knowledge-holders, people with disabilities).
- **Avoid tokenism and stereotypes:** diverse characters have real roles and agency, not just names or costumes. No "saviour" stories in which one group rescues another; show communities solving their own problems.
- **Language:** use inclusive, person-first or identity-first wording as communities prefer; gender-neutral wording where gender isn't relevant; plain words that multilingual learners can follow. Offer translation.
- **Access:** every activity has a way in for learners with visual, hearing, mobility or learning differences, and for learners who don't want to perform, draw or sing. Describe any images in words (alt text) for learners who can't see them.
- **Context and cost:** no assumptions about home resources, internet access, family structure, religion or holidays. Activities must work at little or no cost.
- **Sources:** where possible, include voices and sources from the learners' own region alongside international ones.
- Include a one-line **"Inclusion check"** in the Quality Check of each plan, stating how the plan meets this checklist.
