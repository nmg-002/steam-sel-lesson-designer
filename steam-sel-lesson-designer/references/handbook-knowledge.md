# Handbook knowledge: EMC², the three art forms and the 6-Step Approaches

The core methods of *Keep Calm and STEAM Ahead! Handbook for Educators*, Edition 2 (2026), Chapters 1–4 (first published as Edition 1, 2025). Read this before designing any activity.

**Contents:** 1. SEL and EMC² · 2. STEAM · 3. The three art forms · 4. Pedagogical principles · 5. The 6-Step Approaches · 6. Single-Point Rubric

## 1. SEL and the EMC² framework
- **SEL** helps learners understand and manage emotions, build positive relationships and make responsible decisions. It should be **woven across the curriculum**, not taught as a stand-alone subject.
- **EMC²**, from UNESCO MGIEP, has four pillars. Think of them as "the four corners of a sturdy table":

| Pillar | Meaning | What it looks like in an arts activity |
|---|---|---|
| **E**mpathy | Seeing and feeling from another's perspective | Drawing how a character or object feels, stepping into a role, hearing lived experience in a song |
| **M**indfulness | Being present and aware | Slowing down to observe, listening closely, attending to voice and body while performing |
| **C**ritical Inquiry | Questioning, analysing, evaluating | Breaking a concept into visual parts, analysing why music evokes a mood, questioning a character's motives |
| **C**ompassion | Caring about others' struggles and acting kindly | Personifying a vulnerable being, reflecting on hardship, linking the concept to caring action |

Every plan must meaningfully address **at least one** EMC² pillar. Two to four is better, as long as each is genuine and not a label.

## 2. STEAM
Science (the natural world), Technology (human-made tools and systems), Engineering (design, build, test), Arts (language arts, liberal arts such as history, sociology and political science, and fine arts such as music, drama and visual art), and Mathematics (numbers, patterns, relationships).
The handbook's two integrations: **(1)** bring accessible arts (doodles, soundscapes, role plays) into every subject; **(2)** weave SEL through them.
Metaphor for educators: STEAM without SEL is like training only your arms. SEL plus arts is the full-body workout.

## 3. The three art forms

**A. DOODLING: "Keep Calm and Doodle On"**
- A doodle is a spontaneous, simple, expressive mark that helps you think ("brain fingerprints"). It is **not** a sketch or a technical diagram. Proportion and polish don't matter. Meaning does.
- Learning gains are greatest when **learners make their own visuals**.
- Techniques: basic shapes, then elements, then combine; **personification** (faces, feelings, personalities); speech and thought bubbles; arrows and call-outs; labels; humour and puns (e.g. a "cat-ion" with a "pawsitive" attitude); visual metaphor (the leaf as a chef); mind maps and graphic organisers; a dash of colour for emphasis; sound-effect words ("vroom").
- Bank of example ideas: Emotional Water Cycle; Plant Personalities; Force & Motion characters; Tech Teamwork (CPU and screen talking); Coding Buddies; Perseverant Bridges; Expressive Machines (levers with effortful faces); Emotional Engineering Process; historical figures with speech bubbles; landforms with personalities; character arc as an emotional journey; branches of government as teammates; Fraction Friends (confident halves, nervous quarters); Growth-Mindset Number Line; Supportive Shapes; angles as characters (cute acute, lounging obtuse); alphabet doodles for vocabulary ("g" with grapes growing from it).
- Tools: pencil and paper, chalkboard, or free digital tools (Inkscape, Krita, GIMP).

**B. MUSIC: "Keep Calm and Jam On"**
- Music includes ambient and natural sounds, soundscapes, everyday-object instruments (a water xylophone made from glasses, water and a spoon; tapping pens; household objects), songs with lyrics, instrumentals, and **local and folk music** ("Vocal for Local").
- Uses: **setting the ambience** (calm, focused, tense, joyful), storytelling soundtracks, historical empathy through songs of an era, lyric analysis for language, intertextuality (songs that reference literature), story writing from a soundtrack, mapping folk music across regions, composing and remixing.
- Recurring sequences:
  - *Ambience arc* (e.g. Pythagoras and Greek civilisation): set the scene with period music, then narrate the story over low volume, then keep subtle background during problem-solving, then intensify the mood at moments of conflict, then close with reflective or melancholic music.
  - *Song/music video* (Boiron-inspired): listen without visuals (emotions, story, standout words), then imagine the visuals, then watch the official video and compare, then do linguistic analysis (tenses, adjectives, metaphors), then make SEL connections.
  - *Soundtrack to story*: set context, then play the track (notice tempo and tone shifts), then brainstorm words, images and emotions, then draft a story with constraints (e.g. past tense, one dialogue), then share and reflect on how one track inspired different stories.
- Honour learners who prefer silence. When music doesn't fit (e.g. heavy numericals), use the **"X factor"** instead: a story, anecdote or incident about the people behind the concept.
- Open-licensed sources to point to (by name; do not invent links): Internet Archive, ccMixter, Free Music Archive, Musopen, Public Domain Music, Wiki Songbook, Wikimedia Commons Audio, Pixabay audio. Free editing tool: Audacity.

**C. DRAMATISATION: "Keep Calm and Act On"**
- Forms: **Monologue** (one character's inner thoughts), **Dialogue** (two or more characters; relationships and conflict), **Narration** (a guiding voice with embedded dialogue or monologue), **Tableaux** (frozen body pictures, no words, no materials; inspired by Boal's Image Theatre), **Scene enactment / role play / physical theatre**, **Reflection journal** after staging.
- Anything can be a character: historical figures, ordinary citizens, organs, molecules, numbers, angles, objects ("I am the parasol, and I'm in a closet feeling lonely, longing for my bestie, the sun!").
- Examples: Ashoka after Kalinga (monologue, dialogue, scene, tableaux; then perspectives of a courtier, a citizen, a warrior); a tired stomach lamenting its workload; teeth and tongue discussing digestion; Marie Curie discovering radium; "When Angles Argue"; "Zero to Hero" (zero's monologue on being undervalued); Hydrogen and Oxygen dialogue for water formation.
- Principles: drama is for **exploration, not a polished performance**. Improvise. Share roles across research, script, acting and production. **Balance creative freedom with academic accuracy.**

## 4. Pedagogical principles
- **Inductive** (introduce a concept) or **deductive** (reinforce or review) use, formal or informal settings, one art form or a blend.
- **Start small**: one doodle, one sound clip, one short role play, then reflect ("What did you feel? What new idea came to mind?").
- **Adapt to age**: younger learners get simple drawing, singing and movement; older learners get complex role play, composing, lyric analysis and debate.
- **Inclusion and UDL**: arts give non-verbal routes to participate. They support neurodiversity, multilingual learners and quieter learners.
- **Low-resource and Global South friendly**: every plan must work with paper, pen, voice and bodies. Offer a no-device or no-internet option every time. Draw on local traditions.
- **Plans are outlines, not scripts**: leave space for the class to evolve and for learners' ideas to flourish.
- **Integration objectives only**: state just a couple of do-able objectives specific to the art integration, not the whole syllabus. Success criteria then show how the subject, the art form and SEL growth will be visible in learners' work (see `assessment-and-inclusion.md`).
- **Monitor and adapt**: Is participation broad or limited to a few? Are learners engaged, hesitant or confused? Record observations and adjust next time.
- **Emotional safety**: anticipate triggers (e.g. water scarcity, pollution, war, displacement, loss, illness) and plan opt-outs and gentle debriefs.

## 5. The 6-Step Approaches (the backbone of every output)

**Mode 1: Educator creates a resource** (doodle, soundtrack or script that the educator presents)
1. Choose the concept (subject, concept, grade).
2. Plan it: list 3–5 key elements, decide how to make it relatable (characters, bubbles, metaphor, humour, real-world link). For music, find tracks and note the emotions they evoke. For drama, identify characters.
3. Create it: the doodle description and layout, the track sequence with timestamps for mood changes, or the script.
4. Evaluate it: Is it clear and accurate? Are the details relevant? Does it invite reflection or discussion?
5. Implement it: as an introduction, mood-setter, discussion starter, review or summary; say how learners will interact with or extend it; include EMC² discussion questions.
6. Share and discuss with colleagues or the educator community.

**Mode 2: Learners create** (a lesson plan where learners doodle, use music or act)
1. Objectives: a couple of do-able integration objectives ("Learners will be able to…") plus **materials**.
2. Introduction: introduce or revise the concept.
3. Prompts and instructions: clear, scaffolded prompts. Model the technique first, since learners may not be used to doodling, analysing music or acting. Say whether the work is individual or in groups.
4. SEL integration: state how each chosen EMC² pillar is developed.
5. Evaluate and implement: check alignment with content, that it fits the time available, and that it meets an EMC² goal. Then apply the rubric and plan how to monitor participation.
6. Share and discuss.

**Mode 3: Quick starter** (5–10 minutes): one art micro-activity plus one reflection question.

**Mode 4: Real-world issue / SDG** (Chapter 5): any of the modes above, planned through the **SDG Lens** (`sdg-lens.md`) and ending in a learner action.

## 6. Single-Point Rubric (Glows and Grows)
Use it to self-check every plan. Offer it to the educator for self or peer review.

| Criterion | Target |
|---|---|
| Connection between subject and art form | The art form clearly connects to the concept and is **factually correct** |
| Alignment with SEL outcomes | At least one EMC² goal is **meaningfully** addressed |
| Creativity of the artistic approach | Thoughtful, **age-appropriate**, invites engagement and expression |
| Learner-centred design | Encourages participation from **all** learners, makes room for diverse voices, abilities and emotional responses, and includes reflection that links to real life |
| Feasibility and time management | Realistic for the resources, class time and teacher preparation available |

Feedback language is strengths-based: **"It glows…"** (what shines) and **"It could grow by…"** (specific, kind suggestions).
