# Real-world issues and the SDG Lens ("Keep Calm and Care On")

From Chapter 5 of the handbook, new in Edition 2 (2026). Read this when a lesson links to a real-world issue or an SDG, or when the educator chooses the "Real-world issue" path.

**Contents:** Why · The SDG Lens · Truth, Feelings, Hope · Depth by age · Worked case: climate change · Swap the issue · Then, Now and Future · SDG evaluation checks

**Why.** The 17 Sustainable Development Goals (2030 Agenda, 2015) are already hiding in every syllabus: the water cycle, percentages, a rain poem, the freedom struggle. ("Every lesson is like a cup of chai: look closely and the world is in it.") SDG Target 4.7 asks education to build knowledge and skills for sustainable development, human rights, gender equality, peace, global citizenship and cultural diversity. SDG Target 13.3 names climate education directly. The SDGs are **a thread through every subject**, not a separate subject. Facts alone rarely change hearts. Arts-based SEL makes an issue memorable and gives feelings a safe place to land.

**The SDG Lens: six steps, any issue**
1. **Spot the issue** hiding inside the topic, or showing up in the learners' community.
2. **Find the local story:** whose life it touches, close to home (a grandparent, a farmer, a shopkeeper, a classmate, a river, a tree).
3. **Map the SDGs:** one main goal, plus the two or three connected goals most issues touch.
4. **Pick the art form(s) and create.** Mixing is encouraged here (e.g. open with a soundscape, move to a doodle, close with a tableau).
5. **Build EMC²:** one question or prompt for each pillar.
6. **Act and share:** one small, real action, and who learners will share their work with (an assembly, parents, the panchayat or municipal council). Sharing turns a lesson into a small act of citizenship.

Educators most often skip Step 2 (local story) and Step 6 (action). These are the steps that make an issue feel real and leave learners hopeful rather than helpless, so **never drop them**.

**Handle with care: Truth · Feelings · Hope through action**
- **Truth:** be honest about the issue; don't water it down.
- **Feelings:** make room for worry, anger or sadness; the arts are a gentle way to express them. Many learners live these issues (water shortages, discrimination, poverty, floods). Even distant issues cause real worry: in a 2021 survey of 10,000 people aged 16–25 in 10 countries, 59% were very or extremely worried about climate change, over 45% said it affected daily life, and 75% said the future is frightening (Hickman et al., 2021).
- **Hope through action:** always end with something learners can do, however small, and examples of people already making a difference.
- Offer choices, never force personal disclosures, and know where to refer a learner who needs support. Lived experience is precious material only when the learner chooses to share it.
- *"One concept, one feeling, one action is plenty for a lesson."* Local examples beat faraway ones. **Always close on hope.**

**Match the depth of the issue to age** (works for any SDG)

| Ages | Focus | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| 5–8 | Wonder, love of nature and people, simple care actions | No scary images; focus on "How can we help?" |
| 8–11 | Causes and effects, local changes, everyday choices | Stories and characters; small, concrete numbers |
| 11–14 | Systems, data, fairness, community action | Ask who is affected most, and why |
| 14–18 | Policy, justice, economics, innovation, careers | Welcome debate; connect to real negotiations and solutions |

**Worked case: climate change (SDG 13)**, a "threat multiplier" (a term first used by the CNA Military Advisory Board in 2007) because it makes other problems worse. It is linked to SDG 2 (crops, food prices), 6 (glaciers, wells, saltwater in farms), 7 (solar, wind), 11 (urban heat, flooding), 12 (waste, fast fashion), 14 (oceans, mangroves, coral) and 15 (forests, habitats).
- *Doodle, "The Greenhouse Party"* (ages 8–11, science): the Earth under a blanket, sun rays as visitors, a CO₂/CH₄/N₂O "gas gang" blocking the exit ("Who added another blanket?!"). Learners add the speech bubble of a tree soaking up CO₂. Critical Inquiry: where does the blanket metaphor break down? (A blanket can be removed; CO₂ stays in the air for centuries.)
- *Music, "Listen to the data"* (ages 11–18, maths): data sonification. Map yearly temperatures or CO₂ (about 280 ppm before the Industrial Revolution; about 426 ppm at Mauna Loa in September 2026, per the NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory) to rising notes on a water xylophone, Sa Re Ga Ma Pa, or a keyboard; compare with a graph; ask how hearing the data changed feelings. CO₂ keeps rising, so tell the educator to check the latest NOAA Mauna Loa figure (gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends) before teaching. Also: **warming stripes**, created by climate scientist Professor Ed Hawkins (National Centre for Atmospheric Science, University of Reading) and first shown in 2018, show each year's temperature as a stripe from cool blue to hot red. Hawkins shares them under a Creative Commons licence. Educators can download stripes for their country or region from **showyourstripes.info** and credit them as *"Warming stripes by Ed Hawkins, University of Reading"*. Learners doodle the stripes and play them as a rhythm: soft taps for blue years, loud claps for red. Any stripes the learners draw themselves are illustrations, not real data.
- *Drama, "The Village Council"* (ages 11–18, social sciences): a council meets after a third failed monsoon (farmer, fisherwoman, shopkeeper, teacher, young activist, grandparent, district officer); a dry well's monologue; tableaux of the village 30 years ago, today, and in 2050; narration of the Chipko movement, which began in 1973 in the Himalayan villages of what was then Uttar Pradesh (now Uttarakhand), when villagers, many of them women, hugged trees to stop them being cut down. Journal: whose voice was hardest to play? Who had the most and least power? What felt fair?
- *Mangroves, Goa* (Grade 7, science and geography), all six steps: coastal ecosystems under pressure; fishing families on Chorão island on Goa's Mandovi river, whose western tip is protected as the Dr. Salim Ali Bird Sanctuary (an estuarine mangrove habitat); mangroves defend against storm surges and erosion, act as a nursery for fish and crabs, and store carbon; SDG 13 + 14 + 15; an "Auntie Mangrove" superhero doodle holding back a grumpy wave, a calm-storm-calm soundscape, the monologue "I Am the Mangrove"; EMC² questions ("What would happen to the baby fish if the mangrove disappeared?"); action: "Thank you, Mangrove" posters or a visit with a local naturalist.
- More climate ideas by age: "My Friend the Tree" doodle (5–8); a melting ice cube's monologue (8–11); a carbon atom's journey comic (11–14); a coral reef's blues verse (14–18); "Fraction Forest"; sonified local rainfall; "The Graph on Trial"; a climate alphabet ("M is for Mangrove"); a letter from the Earth and a reply; "Then and Now" maps from elders' memories; Industrial Revolution timeline; "Who pays the price?" doodle; mock climate summit; "The Sun and the Solar Panel" dialogue (young learners); design a school that stays cool without AC; "The Pitch" for a climate solution.

The lens also works for poverty, hunger, health, decent work, migration and disability inclusion. **Start with what learners already care about.**

**"Then, Now and Future" structure** (sample plan *Monsoon Memories: Our Changing Rains*, Grades 6–8, geography + science + languages, 3 × 40 min plus a homework interview, SDG 13 with 6 and 2). Session 1: a rain recording, "What does the monsoon mean to you?", and how warming makes rain less predictable. Homework: interview an elder (when the rains came, what changed, songs or sayings). Sessions 2–3: a folded-page doodle (Then | Now, with faces on clouds, river and fields); a group object soundscape ("Monsoon Then" vs "Monsoon Now"); three tableaux (Then · Now · 2050: The Future We Choose), with a tap-on-the-shoulder thought; action: each group turns one adaptation idea (rainwater harvesting, planting trees, reviving a pond) into a poster or a proposal to the school or council. Critical Inquiry: compare memories with local rainfall data. Adapting: ages 5–8 skip the interview and use body rain sounds; 8–11 use a three-question interview; 14–18 add sonified rainfall data and a mock panchayat hearing. **Swap it:** interview elders about girls' schooling (SDG 5), how the village got water (SDG 6), or what people threw away 50 years ago (SDG 12).

**SDG evaluation checks** (in addition to the Single-Point Rubric in `handbook-knowledge.md`):
- Is the information accurate?
- Does it connect to learners' lives or local context?
- Does it invite reflection **without causing undue distress**?
- Does it point towards **hope or action** / balance honesty with hope and agency?
- After teaching: were learners engaged, or anxious and withdrawn? Did the session end on a hopeful note?

Key terms: adaptation, mitigation, carbon sink, greenhouse gases, data sonification, eco-anxiety, global citizenship.
